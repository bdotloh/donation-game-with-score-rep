EMPIRICAL ANALYSIS OF TWO-REPUTATION-STATE TRANSITION GROUPS
=============================================================

Purpose
-------

This analysis checks proposed limiting behavior for score-distribution Markov
chains grouped by whether their initial transition probabilities alpha, beta,
and gamma are zero or positive.

The results are empirical numerical evidence, not mathematical proofs.


1. Fixed experimental setup
---------------------------

The analysis used:

  - Two reputation states.
  - CmeetsN enabled.
  - NmeetsC fixed to all zeros.
  - Initial score distribution:

        w_0 = [0.5, 0.5, 0, ..., 0]

    Therefore P(w_0 = 0) = 0.5 and P(w_0 = 1) = 0.5.

  - Maximum score: maxw = 100.
  - Every valid CmeetsN rule: 1,296 rules.
  - Every binary strategy: 16 strategies.
  - Total combinations: 1,296 * 16 = 20,736.


2. How the combinations were initially grouped
----------------------------------------------

For every (BigB, strategy) combination:

  1. Parse the BigB update rule and strategy.
  2. Calculate alpha(w_0), beta(w_0), and gamma(w_0).
  3. Classify each rate as either exactly zero or positive.
  4. Assign the combination to one of eight groups.

The eight group sizes were:

  a. alpha=0, beta=0, gamma=0:       576
  b. alpha=0, beta>0, gamma=0:     1,728
  c. alpha>0, beta=0, gamma=0:     1,728
  d. alpha>0, beta>0, gamma=0:     5,184
  e. alpha=0, beta=0, gamma>0:     1,728
  f. alpha=0, beta>0, gamma>0:     1,152
  g. alpha>0, beta=0, gamma>0:     5,184
  h. alpha>0, beta>0, gamma>0:     3,456


3. How the limiting claims were checked
---------------------------------------

The initial 500-step batch was not sufficient to reliably identify all limits,
because some chains converge algebraically slowly.

For the limit checks:

  1. Each chain was represented using a fixed score vector of length 101,
     corresponding to scores 0 through 100.
  2. Each chain was evolved for up to 100,000 steps.
  3. A chain was marked stationary if its largest one-step probability change
     was at most 1e-12.
  4. A proposed limiting condition was marked as numerically satisfied when
     its error was at most 1e-6.

The rates depend only on P(w_t=0), and each rate is affine in P(w_t=0).
Therefore, the rate functions are completely identified by their values at
P(w_t=0)=0 and P(w_t=0)=1.

Using these endpoint values showed that the 20,736 combinations reduce to only
36 unique nonlinear rate functions. The 36 unique dynamics were solved, and
their results were mapped back to all 20,736 combinations.


4. Results for the proposed hypotheses
--------------------------------------

a. alpha=0, beta=0, gamma=0

Expected:

  P(w_t=n) remains equal to P(w_0=n) for every score n.

Result:

  Empirically true for all 576 combinations.
  The distribution remains exactly [0.5, 0.5, 0, ..., 0].


b. alpha=0, beta>0, gamma=0

Expected:

  lim P(w_t=0) = P(w_0=0) = 0.5
  lim P(w_t=maxw) = 1 - P(w_0=0) = 0.5

Result:

  Empirically true for all 1,728 combinations.
  Final P(0) was 0.5 and final P(100) was approximately 0.5.


c. alpha>0, beta=0, gamma=0

Expected:

  lim P(w_t=1) = P(w_0=0) + P(w_0=1) = 1
  Scores n>=2 retain their initial mass.

Result:

  Consistent with the hypothesis for all 1,728 combinations.

  Of these, 1,152 reached the strict stationary threshold. The remaining 576
  converged very slowly. After 100,000 steps, their remaining P(0) was about
  0.000039993, with the rest at score 1.

  There were no observed counterexamples. The numerical residual is consistent
  with slow convergence toward the proposed limit.


d. alpha>0, beta>0, gamma=0

Expected:

  lim P(w_t=maxw) = 1.

Result:

  False in general.

  Only 2,304 of 5,184 combinations satisfied P(maxw)=1 within the numerical
  criterion. There are genuine converged counterexamples.

  The reason is that beta(w_0)>0 only describes beta at the initial
  distribution. For some combinations, beta(w_t) approaches zero as P(w_t=0)
  approaches zero. Upward movement then stops before all mass reaches maxw.

  Observed counterexample outcomes included distributions frozen over low
  scores with:

    - Mean score approximately 1.357143 and effective support through score 6.
    - Mean score approximately 1.5 and effective support through score 7.

  Other cases did move all mass to maxw.


e. alpha=0, beta=0, gamma>0

Expected:

  lim P(w_t=0) = 1.

Result:

  Consistent with the hypothesis for all 1,728 combinations.

  Of these, 1,152 reached the strict stationary threshold. The remaining 576
  converged slowly. After 100,000 steps, their remaining mass above zero was
  about 0.000039993.

  There were no observed counterexamples.


f. alpha=0, beta>0, gamma>0

Expected:

  lim P(w_t=0) = P(w_0=0) + y,
  where y is between 0 and 1-P(w_0=0).

Result:

  Empirically true for all 1,152 combinations.

  There were only two distinct stationary outcomes:

    1. P(0) approximately 1.

       All mass eventually moved to score 0.

    2. P(0) approximately 0.749478659,
       P(maxw) approximately 0.166781875,
       mean score approximately 24.92635.

       This is a mixed boundary outcome: substantial mass remains at score 0,
       while substantial mass reaches maxw.


g. alpha>0, beta=0, gamma>0

Expected:

  The support of the distribution remains fixed by the initial distribution.

Result:

  Empirically true for all 5,184 combinations.

  Because beta is zero, no mass can move from score 1 to score 2. Starting from
  support {0,1}, the support remains within {0,1}.

  Multiple equilibria occur. Observed limiting P(0) values were approximately:

    - 0
    - 0.381966011
    - 0.5
    - 0.618033989
    - 1

  The values 0.381966011 and 0.618033989 are the golden-ratio conjugate values:

    (3 - sqrt(5)) / 2 approximately 0.381966011
    (sqrt(5) - 1) / 2 approximately 0.618033989

  Some cases approach 0 or 1 only algebraically slowly.


h. alpha>0, beta>0, gamma>0

The original list repeated the g condition for h. This analysis treats h as
the missing eighth case in which all three initial rates are positive.

Result:

  Initial positivity of all rates does not determine a unique limiting form.
  Six distinct rate-function behaviors were observed.

  Three broad outcomes occurred:

    1. All mass moved to maxw.

    2. Mass approached maxw very slowly.

    3. The chain converged to a low-score stationary distribution rather than
       maxw.

  The low-score stationary outcomes included:

    - P(0) approximately 0.292893219,
      mean score approximately 1.207107.

    - P(0) approximately 1/3,
      mean score approximately 4/3.

    - P(0) approximately 0.381966011,
      mean score approximately 1.618034.


5. Additional observations beyond the hypotheses
-------------------------------------------------

Observation 1: Initial rate signs are not enough to determine the limit.

The groups use alpha(w_0), beta(w_0), and gamma(w_0). However, the rates change
with P(w_t=0). A rate that is initially positive can later approach zero.

This explains why hypothesis d fails and why h has several different outcomes.
A stronger classification should include the full rate functions, or at least
their endpoint values at P(w_t=0)=0 and P(w_t=0)=1.


Observation 2: There are only 36 unique dynamics among 20,736 combinations.

Many different BigB and strategy combinations generate exactly the same alpha,
beta, and gamma functions. Grouping by the endpoint rate signature greatly
reduces the analysis without changing the induced score dynamics.


Observation 3: Some convergence is algebraically slow.

Several groups contain chains that had not reached the strict 1e-12 stationary
threshold after 100,000 steps, even though their behavior consistently
approached the proposed limit.

For example, residual masses of approximately 0.000039993 remained in some c
and e chains. This is why finite-time results should not automatically be
interpreted as limiting counterexamples.


Observation 4: Zero beta creates an invariant support boundary.

When beta=0 throughout the trajectory, mass cannot move upward from positive
scores. For the chosen initial distribution supported on {0,1}, the support
therefore remains within {0,1}. This gives a structural explanation for g.


Observation 5: Boundary mixtures can persist.

Group f contains a stationary outcome with mass at both score 0 and maxw.
Therefore, having both beta and gamma initially positive does not imply that
the stationary distribution is concentrated in the interior.


Observation 6: Golden-ratio-related equilibria appear repeatedly.

The values near 0.381966 and 0.618034 occur in group g, and 0.381966 also
appears in group h. These values arise from fixed points of the nonlinear
rate/distribution feedback, rather than from numerical noise.


Observation 7: maxw affects boundary-concentrated outcomes.

Outcomes that accumulate mass at score 100 depend on the imposed maxw=100
boundary. Low-score equilibria and invariant-support outcomes are less
sensitive to maxw, provided maxw remains above their effective support.
