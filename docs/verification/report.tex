\section{What was tested}

The engine computes, for a well-mixed population of $n$ strategies, the stable score distribution of each strategy as the stationary distribution of a non-linear birth--death chain (notes, Eqs.~1--6), with transition rates
\[
g_j^{(i)} = (1-\epsilon)\,G_j^{(i)} + \epsilon\,\epsilon_\uparrow,\qquad
f_j^{(i)} = (1-\epsilon)\,F_j^{(i)} + \epsilon\,\epsilon_\downarrow,\qquad
\epsilon_\uparrow+\epsilon_\downarrow = 1 .
\]
Strategies are binary (score 0 or at least 1), ternary with a cut-off $\tau$ (score 0, $1$ to $\tau$, above $\tau$) or $\phi$-Disc (help a receiver with a positive score with probability $j^{-\phi}$, where $j$ is the donor's score); every decision point takes a probability in $[0,1]$. Scoring norms are Frean and Marsland barcodes, binary (8 characters) or ternary (18 characters). Scores have no upper limit; the chains run until they converge or reach a maximum number of steps. On top of this the repository has the replicator dynamics (the score chains restart from the initial distribution at every step), the rest-point finder, the Dash GUI, an ESS sweep for Slurm, and the earlier scripts moved onto the engine.

\section{Binary engine against the Julia code}

The user's Julia code is the reference for everything binary. The script \texttt{make\_reference.jl} runs \texttt{helper\_funcs.jl} and saves its output as tab-separated files in \texttt{tests/reference/}. The \texttt{OrderedCollections} package, which was not available, is replaced by \texttt{Dict} for this.

\textbf{Stable scores.} \texttt{stable\_cases.tsv} holds 200 one-strategy and 200 two-strategy cases, with noise 0 (203 cases), 0.01 (93) and 0.1 (104), and \texttt{maxn} in $\{0, 1, 7, 100, 1000\}$. For each case the engine's final score distributions, payoffs and the three rates $\alpha=g_1$, $\beta=g_j$, $\gamma=f_j$ are compared with Julia's, with an absolute tolerance of $10^{-12}$. All 400 cases agree. Payoffs follow Julia's convention: for one strategy they are computed from the distribution before the last step, for two strategies from the final one.

\textbf{ESS.} \texttt{ess\_sweep.tsv} holds the ESS that \texttt{find\_ESS} finds among the 16 binary strategies for 1596 norms at noise 0 and at noise 0.01 (3192 rows, 809 ESS in total, with $\rho_A=0.99$, \texttt{maxn}~100 and fitness threshold $10^{-5}$). \texttt{tests/reference/compare\_ess.py} compares every row and finds the same ESS lists in all 3192; the test suite checks a fixed random sample of 60 rows.

\textbf{ESS sweep script.} \texttt{scripts/ess\_sweep.py}, which runs the same search for any norm set (and is what runs on R\=apoi), was run on all 1296 CN-only binary norms at noise 0 and 0.01 and gave the same ESS lists as Julia for every norm. The run--resume--merge cycle and the Julia agreement on a small set are part of the test suite.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\phi$}{phi}-Disc (pages 7--8)}

Settings: \texttt{CN:00000-0+ NC:00000000}, a population made only of $\phi$-Disc players.

\textbf{Rates.} The notes give $G_1=0$, $G_j=\dfrac{1-w_0}{4(j-1)^{\phi}}$ for $j>1$, $F_j=\dfrac{1-w_0}{4}\Bigl(1-\dfrac{1}{j^{\phi}}\Bigr)$ for $j\ge1$, and $g_1=\epsilon\epsilon_\uparrow$. The engine's $g_j$ and $f_j$ were compared with these (and Eqs.~3--4) for $\phi\in\{0, 0.17, 0.5, 1, 2\}$ on a score distribution with support on scores 0 to 6: the largest difference is $5.6\times10^{-17}$. For $\phi=0$ the rates equal those of the Discriminators exactly (difference 0), as the notes say.

\textbf{The case $\phi=1$.} With $\epsilon=0.1$, $\epsilon_\uparrow=0.4$, $\epsilon_\downarrow=0.6$, the notes solve the normalisation equation for $\pi_0$ and give
\[
\pi_k=\pi_0\,\frac{\tilde\epsilon_\uparrow}{\tilde\epsilon_\downarrow}\,k\,r^{k-1}\,\frac{\Gamma(k+a)\,\Gamma(1+b)}{\Gamma(1+a)\,\Gamma(k+b)},
\qquad a=\frac{B}{\tilde\epsilon_\uparrow},\; b=\frac{\tilde\epsilon_\downarrow}{B+\tilde\epsilon_\downarrow},\; r=\frac{\tilde\epsilon_\uparrow}{B+\tilde\epsilon_\downarrow},
\]
with $B=(1-\epsilon)(1-\pi_0)/4$, $\tilde\epsilon_\uparrow=\epsilon\epsilon_\uparrow$ and $\tilde\epsilon_\downarrow=\epsilon\epsilon_\downarrow$.

\subsection{The norm printed on page 7}

The barcode lists the 8 entries in the order (needy player's class, whether the donor helped, donor's class). \texttt{CN:00000-00} has its minus sign at (positive, declined, positive): a donor with a positive score loses one when she does not help a needy player with a positive score, which is the norm of page 5. The $\phi$-Disc norm adds a plus at (positive, helped, positive), giving \texttt{CN:00000-0+}. The printed \texttt{CN:000000-+} would put the minus at (positive, helped, score-0 donor), which both contradicts the sentence next to it and is not a legal norm (a score-0 player cannot lose a score; the engine rejects it).

\section{Ternary classes and \texorpdfstring{$\phi$}{phi}-Disc beyond the notes}
\label{sec:independent}

\subsection{Checks that were already in the suite}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Lifted binary norms and strategies.} A ternary norm and ternary strategies that ignore the split at $\tau$ must give exactly the binary results. This is tested for $\tau\in\{1,2,5\}$ on a set of binary norms (\texttt{test\_ternary\_that\_\allowbreak ignores\_tau\_matches\_binary}), and an ESS sweep of the binary CN-only norms lifted to ternary agreed with the binary sweep, with no differences.
\item \emph{A ternary strategy does use $\tau$:} a strategy that treats scores $1..\tau$ differently from scores above $\tau$ gives a different answer when $\tau$ moves (\texttt{test\_ternary\_\allowbreak strategy\_uses\_tau}).
\item \emph{Probabilities.} Rates and payoffs are linear in each help probability; probability is conserved by every step; the stationary distribution satisfies detailed balance (Eqs.~5--6); chains whose up-rate at the top score is at least the down-rate are flagged as not converged.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Independent verification}

Three independent checks were made after the engine was finished. The people (or agents) writing them did not use the engine's code to build their expected values.

\textbf{A blind re-implementation.} A second implementation (\texttt{tests/independent\_model.py}, 137 lines) was written from the notes and \texttt{helper\_funcs.jl} before the engine's source was opened. It computes $G$, $F$, $g$ and $f$ by direct enumeration (loops over the other player's strategy and score, the roles, and whether help is given) and iterates the chains itself. It reproduces the Julia outputs on 345 binary cases to $1.8\times10^{-15}$ and the numbers of pages 4, 6, 7 and 8. Against the engine:
\begin{itemize}
\item 5000 random configurations of rates (binary, ternary and lifted norms; $\tau\in\{1,2,3,5,8\}$; one to three strategies of mixed kinds including probabilistic tables and $\phi$-Disc with $\phi\in[0,3]$; $\epsilon\in\{0,0.1,0.5,1\}$; shares of zero; mass beyond $\tau$ and mass only in the top class): largest difference $2.2\times10^{-16}$;
\item 1780 chains of 25 to 400 steps: $1.1\times10^{-15}$, with mass conserved;
\item stationary distributions: the independent fixed point of Eqs.~5--6 is unchanged by one engine step to $5\times10^{-15}$ on 227 configurations, and the engine's own converged runs agree to $5\times10^{-8}$ (set by its stopping rule);
\item the three-segment closed form for a single class-based ternary strategy (100 cases), the $\phi$-Disc rates of page~7 (1000 cases, $1.4\times10^{-16}$), the gamma-function solution of page~8 for six noise settings ($2.8\times10^{-16}$), $\phi=0$ against the Discriminators (identical), and strategies whose classes differ from the norm's.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{An agent-based simulation.} A population of $10^6$ agents was simulated straight from the verbal model: each step every agent meets a random partner, roles are drawn, the Can helps with the strategy's probability, scores move by the norm, and with probability $\epsilon$ the move is replaced by noise. The score histograms were compared with the engine after the same number of steps, bin by bin with binomial standard errors. In 470 comparisons (the two example runs, $\phi$-Disc alone, Defectors against Discriminators, random ternary norms with probabilistic strategies, a mix of $\phi$-Disc and a ternary strategy) 468 were within five standard errors, and the total variation distances were at the level of pure sampling noise; the deviation falls as $N^{-1/2}$. The same yardsticks detect a 10\% change in $\epsilon$, a 4\% change in the role factor, and noise applied only when an interaction happens. The two exceptions are a random case that the engine itself flags as drifting, where the default stopping tolerance leaves the engine short of the stationary law (see below).

\textbf{Hand-computed cases and planted bugs.} Eighteen small cases with $\tau=1$ and $2$ and a few occupied scores were worked out on paper from the notes (CN against NC, the recipient's index against the donor's, the class boundary at score $\tau$ and $\tau+1$, the noise arithmetic, and the exponent of $\phi$-Disc being the donor's score minus one for $G_j$); the engine matches them to $5.6\times10^{-17}$. Bugs were then planted in copies of the engine (a wrong cut-off, the wrong class for the donor, a transposed table, a wrong lift of a binary strategy to ternary, $\phi$-Disc helping a recipient without a score) and each suite was run against them. The existing engine tests missed three cut-off bugs (the engine tests alone do not guard the boundary at $\tau$); the independent tests and the hand cases catch all of them, and the engine tests catch the lift and transposition bugs that the simulation tests miss. All three suites are now in the repository (\texttt{test\_independent\_model.py}, 19 tests; \texttt{test\_agent\_based.py}, 12 tests at $10^5$ agents; \texttt{test\_hand\_cases.py}), so each covers the gaps of the others.

\textbf{What this found.} No error in the rates, the norms, the strategies or the chains. Four points came up:
\begin{itemize}
\item At the default tolerance ($10^{-5}$ on the change in one step) the engine stops before the stationary law for slowly relaxing chains, and the Julia code does the same, so results quoted at the default tolerance are approximate; the simulation sides with the tightly converged distribution.
\item \texttt{Noise} accepted $\epsilon_\uparrow+\epsilon_\downarrow>1$ and then produced negative ``probabilities''. It now refuses a sum above 1 (a sum below 1 is allowed; the notes take it to be 1).
\item The README said a chain is drifting when the up-rate at the top score is at least the down-rate. When both are zero nothing can move and the engine, sensibly, does not call it drifting; the wording is corrected. Also, drifting does not strictly mean there is no stable distribution: in one configuration a stable distribution exists (it was found in closed form and is unchanged by an engine step) but the engine stayed in a slow drifting state for more than $3\times10^4$ steps.
\item The two verifiers share the verbal model with the engine, so a misreading of the notes common to all of them would not be seen. The binary case is anchored to the Julia code. For ternary strategies and norms the conventions (the cut-off at $\tau$, the order of the 18 characters, the orientation of the strategy table) rest on the description given for the engine and on the lifting identity, not on any independent reference.
\end{itemize}

\section{Replicator dynamics, rest points and the GUI}

There is no external reference for the replicator dynamics, so they are tested against closed forms and against games whose answers are known.

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{tests/test\_replicator.py} (7 tests): the payoff matrix reproduces payoffs; Cooperators against Defectors follows the analytic solution; restarting the chains each step matches warm-starting when the scores settle; slow and fast time scales approach the quasi-static limit; the restart keeps the score range bounded; extinct strategies stay extinct and rest points stop the run; the role factor scales the interaction rates.
\item \texttt{tests/test\_simplex.py} (19 tests): the simplex grid; rest points and their stability for the prisoner's dilemma, hawk--dove, two- and three-strategy coordination games and rock--paper--scissors (stable and unstable versions); rest points of the engine's own payoffs against the invasion fitness; the awkward cases (an interior rest point close to an edge, segments of rest points, a zero eigenvalue, an eigenvalue close to the boundary, an edge point next to a vertex); parallel evaluation against serial.
\item \texttt{tests/test\_gui\_logic.py} (47 tests): parsing and validation of every input, building strategies and norms, settings files, presets, and which strategies are shown at a given point of the simplex.
\item Two browser tests (Chromium through Playwright, run with \texttt{-m e2e}) click through all three pages: choose presets, type a ternary strategy, fill in shares, scores, norm and noise, run, click the simplex, enter a point on the boundary (the absent strategy leaves the table, histogram and matrices), play the trajectory and download the data. A second test loads a settings file for two strategies. These were last run before the migration commits, which do not touch the GUI.
\end{itemize}

Reviews during development found and fixed: the order of the ternary strategy grid, a broken state when toggling between binary and ternary norms, missing validation of settings files, infinite or NaN inputs, a cap on the initial score, the name of a preset, thread-safety of the rate caches, round-off giving slightly negative rates, and several rest-point cases.

\section{The earlier scripts on the engine}

For each, the old script was run on the same inputs as the new one, and the new one was compared with the results already committed in the repository. One difference is deliberate: the old code capped scores at \texttt{maxw} (mass at the cap could only move down), and the engine has no cap. \section{What has not been verified}

\begin{itemize}
\item The notes have no ternary result and give a closed form for $\phi$-Disc only at $\phi=1$. For other $\phi$ the engine agrees with the rates in the notes, but no stationary distribution has been compared with a closed form.
\item The role factor $q(1-q)$ is tested against its definition and by scaling, but Julia only ever uses $q=1/2$.
\item The replicator dynamics, the rest-point classification and the GUI have no reference implementation; they rest on the closed forms and games above.
\item Chains with no stable distribution (for example the money norm with $\epsilon_\uparrow=\epsilon_\downarrow$) are reported after a fixed number of steps, so their values depend on the initial distribution and the number of steps.
\item The Slurm scripts in \texttt{scripts/raapoi/} have been tested locally, with the Slurm environment variables set by hand, but have not been run on R\=apoi itself.
\end{itemize}
